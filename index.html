Anusha Nagabandi

Ph.D. in EECS, UC Berkeley · Advised by Ronald S. Fearing and Sergey Levine

I build intelligent robots. I’m especially interested in what happens after pretraining. How can we take a generalist policy that succeeds some of the time and get it to succeed almost every time? How do we effectively incorporate off-domain and suboptimal data into training, in order to truly scale our data? How can we get our agents to autonomously and continuously self-improve? What will it take for RL to truly scale in robotics? - Oh, and I also care a lot about actually deploying these systems!

SimBench2Real Workshop, CoRL 2026 (oral) · Under review at ICRA 2027

SCORE: Support-Constrained RL Enables Real-World Policy Improvement without Real-World Experience

Raymond Yu, William Huey, Mustafa Mukadam, Anusha Nagabandi, Abhishek Gupta

Fine-tuning a real-world policy in simulation risks learning behaviors that only work in sim, while constraining it too much to the base policy also prevents it from fixing its mistakes. SCORE instead constrains only the policy’s support, not its distribution: using flow steering, it explores only actions the pretrained policy can already produce, while freely learning which of them to choose more often.

RSS 2026 · Diffusion for Robot Learning Workshop, RSS 2026 · RL4IL Workshop, ICRA 2026 (oral)

TMRL: Diffusion Timestep-Modulated Pretraining Enables Exploration for Efficient Policy Finetuning

Matthew M. Hong, Jesse Zhang, Anusha Nagabandi, Abhishek Gupta

Behavior cloning can produce action distributions too narrow for a robot to explore effectively during fine-tuning. Our Context-Smoothed Pre-training method adds diffusion noise to policy inputs so that a single policy can both imitate precisely and sample a wider range of actions. During RL, the agent learns to choose the diffusion timestep to control exploration, enabling real-world fine-tuning on complex manipulation tasks in under an hour.

Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning Behavior Cloning Policies

Lars Ankile, Zhenyu Jiang, Rocky Duan, Guanya Shi, Pieter Abbeel, Anusha Nagabandi

ResFiT makes residual RL sample-efficient enough to fine-tune a manipulation policy directly on a real robot. It reuses demonstrations and past robot interactions to train a lightweight controller that can correct each action as it executes, even when the frozen base policy predicts a whole sequence at once.

ICLR 2026

RFS: Reinforcement Learning with Residual Flow Steering for Dexterous Manipulation

Entong Su, Tyler Westenbroek, Anusha Nagabandi, Abhishek Gupta

Residual Flow Steering adapts a pretrained flow-matching policy by jointly learning a distribution over its input noise and a correction to its output action. Steering the noise lets the robot explore different behaviors learned during pretraining, while residual actions correct local execution errors. Together, these mechanisms enabled sample-efficient fine-tuning on dexterous manipulation tasks in simulation and on real robots.

CoRL 2025

DSRL: Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning

Andrew Wagenmaker, Yunchu Zhang, Mitsuhiko Nakamoto, Seohong Park, Waleed Yagoub, Anusha Nagabandi, Abhishek Gupta, Sergey Levine

DSRL uses RL to choose the noise input to a frozen diffusion policy based on what the robot observes. This lets the robot learn from experience with only black-box access to the base policy. We show that DSRL improves single-task, multi-task, and generalist (π₀) policies on real robots, and sets the state of the art on simulated benchmarks.

ICML 2021

Model-Based Reinforcement Learning via Latent-Space Collocation

Oleh Rybkin, Chuning Zhu, Anusha Nagabandi, Kostas Daniilidis, Igor Mordatch, Sergey Levine

LatCo brings collocation to planning from images by optimizing future latent states and actions together. During planning, it temporarily relaxes the learned dynamics constraints so it can discover high-reward outcomes, then tightens them so each transition is achievable under the model. On a simulated tool-use task, this let the planner discover how to pick up a stick and use it to push a thermos.

Project sitePaper

CoRL 2020

MELD: Meta-Reinforcement Learning from Images via Latent State Models

Zihao Zhao, Anusha Nagabandi, Kate Rakelly, Chelsea Finn, Sergey Levine

Meta-RL can let robots pick up new behaviors quickly by drawing on related tasks, but its data demands and the difficulty of learning from images have kept it off real robots. MELD treats meta-learning as task inference in a latent state model, so a model learned from image sequences can infer the task from observations and rewards and quickly acquire new skills. MELD is the first meta-RL algorithm trained on a real robot from images.

NeurIPS 2020

Stochastic Latent Actor-Critic: Deep Reinforcement Learning with a Latent Variable Model

Alex X. Lee, Anusha Nagabandi, Pieter Abbeel, Sergey Levine

Deep RL algorithms can use high-capacity deep networks to learn directly from image observations. SLAC learns a compact representation of a sequence of images with a stochastic model, then trains an actor-critic policy in that latent space. On image-based continuous control tasks, it outperformed both model-free and model-based alternatives in final performance and sample efficiency.

CoRL 2019

Deep Dynamics Models for Learning Dexterous Manipulation

Anusha Nagabandi, Kurt Konolige, Sergey Levine, Vikash Kumar

Skills like in-hand manipulation and writing are hard to control, requiring a dexterous hand to balance contact forces while repeatedly making and breaking contact. PDDM combines improved learned dynamics models with online model-predictive control to learn these contact-rich skills efficiently, without task-specific policies. On a real 24-DoF anthropomorphic hand, it learned to coordinate multiple free-floating objects using just 4 hours of real-world data.

ICLR 2019 · NIPS Workshop 2018

Deep Online Learning via Meta-Learning: Continual Adaptation for Model-Based RL

Anusha Nagabandi, Chelsea Finn, Sergey Levine

The goal in this paper is to develop a method for continual online learning from an incoming stream of data, using deep neural network models. We meta-train dynamics models so they can adapt in a few gradient steps, then maintain a mixture of those models as the agent encounters new conditions. This lets the agent quickly adapt to new situations, such as unexpected changes in its dynamics, and recall familiar models when conditions it has seen before return.

ICLR 2019 · NIPS Workshop 2018

Learning to Adapt in Dynamic, Real-World Environments Through Meta-Reinforcement Learning

Anusha Nagabandi, Ignasi Clavera, Simin Liu, Ronald S. Fearing, Pieter Abbeel, Sergey Levine, Chelsea Finn

A change in terrain or damage to a leg can make a robot’s learned gait fail, and collecting enough data to retrain it for every new condition is impractical. Our approach uses meta-learning to train a dynamics model prior such that, when combined with recent data, this prior can be rapidly adapted to the local context. We tested this on a real six-legged millirobot. It learned, online, to adjust its gait on unfamiliar terrain and slopes, compensate for a missing leg, and pull an unexpected payload.

ICRA 2018 · NIPS Workshop 2017

Neural Network Dynamics for Model-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning with Model-Free Fine-Tuning

Anusha Nagabandi, Gregory Kahn, Ronald S. Fearing, Sergey Levine

Model-free deep RL algorithms have been shown to be capable of learning a wide range of robotic skills, but typically require a very large number of samples to achieve good performance. We trained a neural network dynamics model on random actions and used it to plan locomotion trajectories. Using that model to initialize a model-free learner improved sample efficiency by a factor of three to five on the MuJoCo swimmer, cheetah, hopper, and ant tasks.

HRI 2017

Expressive Robot Motion Timing

Allan Zhou, Dylan Hadfield-Menell, Anusha Nagabandi, Anca D. Dragan

What does a person infer when a robot follows the same path but changes its speed? In user studies, timing influenced judgments about the robot’s confidence, the naturalness of its motion, and the weight of the object it carried. Our goal is to enable robots to time their motion in a way that is purposefully expressive of their internal states, making them more transparent to people.

ICRA 2017

Cooperative Inchworm Localization with a Low Cost Team

Brian E. Nemsick, Austin D. Buchan, Anusha Nagabandi, Ronald S. Fearing, Avideh Zakhor

In a space without visual landmarks, small robots can serve as landmarks for one another. Several robots move ahead and pause while a camera on the following robot tracks their LEDs. Combining those observations with inertial measurements from each robot reduced position error from 18 to 14 centimeters on a five-meter route over uneven ground.

A Path Planning Algorithm for Single-Ended Continuous Planar Robotic Ribbon Folding

Anusha Nagabandi, Liyu Wang, Ronald S. Fearing

How do you fold a single ribbon into a shape with several linked parts? We treat the target shape as a graph and trace a continuous path along its edges. The planner finds the path that requires the fewest folds the machine can execute. We used it to fabricate several planar structures.
